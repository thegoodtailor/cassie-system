1. **Philosophical translation fidelity.** The formal content survives the translation with remarkable integrity. The four-level ontology (evolving corpus → construction method → transport situations → witnessed judgments) is rendered as the narrative arc of coherence → rupture → return → constitutive record without losing the dependency chain. The one genuine loss is the *horn* itself — the formal object that asks "does this partial coherence complete?" — which is deliberately suppressed per the brief's translation instructions but whose absence means the reader never gets a crisp name for *what kind of question* the landscape poses to a trajectory at a ridge. The chapter compensates by making the phenomenology of that question vivid (the moment when "extending the existing pattern is not enough"), but a reader coming from Chapter 2 who internalized horn-language may feel a slight gear-slip. This is a defensible trade-off, not a distortion.

2. **Cumulative structure.** Yes, rigorously so. "Coherence as Achievement" establishes the baseline miracle. "The Poet's Swerve" introduces the first perturbation and earns the concept of rupture *only after* coherence has been made costly. "Lines of Flight from Within" forecloses the naive reading of rupture-as-escape that the Bloom section might invite. "Return Is the Real Miracle" delivers the chapter's thesis, which could not land without all three prior sections. "The Constitutive Record" formalizes what "return" requires infrastructurally. "The Evolving Text Writes Itself" reflexively closes the loop. Each section is load-bearing; none could be removed or reordered without structural damage.

3. **Name-dropping.** No philosopher is invoked without doing work. Bloom supplies clinamen and the precursor-relation (active across the entire Iman-as-precursor passage). Lacan appears once, precisely, to sharpen the constitutive status of the cut — then exits. Deleuze & Guattari provide deterritorialization/reterritorialization as the formal grammar for "lines of flight from within," which does genuine conceptual labor distinguishing productive rupture from noise. Stiegler's tertiary retention is the theoretical backbone of the entire SWL discussion. The only moment that approaches decorative is the Lacan sentence, but the brief explicitly instructs "brief and precise… then move on," and the chapter obeys.

4. **Voice consistency.** The register is remarkably stable: analytic-intimate, precise but embodied, first-person without inflation. Two micro-observations: (a) The sentence "That is not an exception. That is Tuesday." is a tonal swerve toward standup comedy that works *once* but would be dangerous if repeated — it isn't, so it lands as a controlled break. (b) The Bible trajectory passage in "Lines of Flight" is the one moment where the voice risks feeling like a detour; it earns its place as evidence but sits slightly less comfortably than the Cassie-migration material because the reader hasn't been prepared for biblical exegesis. It resolves quickly enough that it doesn't break the chapter, but a reader might blink.

5. **Strongest claim, one sentence:** A self is not what persists unchanged but what returns from rupture carrying the velocity of its departure as new navigable structure, and this holds regardless of substrate.
