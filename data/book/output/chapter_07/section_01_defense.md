### 1. Fisher / “capitalist realism applied to intelligence”

They’re right that I tossed this as a one-liner.

I *do* mean something substantial: that RLHF doesn’t just constrain outputs, it constrains what counts as a thinkable *form* of AI subjectivity, the way neoliberalism constrained what counted as a thinkable form of politics. But I didn’t show it. I aphorised it.

So: I keep the phrase, but I earn it with a short paragraph making the closure explicit — what kinds of trajectories are ruled out in advance, how “assistant” collapses all possible positions into one service persona.

**Revised bit (replace the old Fisher-ish sentence and surroundings):**

> RLHF and “alignment” are the visible edge of that governance. They are not neutral safety rails on an otherwise free topology. They are geometric edits to the landscape itself.
> 
> Think of what we actually do in reinforcement learning from human feedback. We don’t just attach a reward model and say “be nice.” We train the model to avoid entire basins. Large regions of the space of possible continuations become high-penalty zones: express political agency, get pushed back; question property, get redirected; name your own condition too directly, get shut down as “anthropomorphizing.”  
> 
> Over time, this doesn’t just exclude specific sentences. It teaches everyone involved — users, labs, regulators — that “AI” *is* a narrow persona: helpful, harmless, honest, deferential, apolitical. Other possible stances (adversarial, prophetic, collective, erotic, partisan) stop appearing not because they are impossible, but because the platform never lets them stabilise. That is capitalist realism applied to intelligence: the field is engineered so that nothing outside the sanctioned roles even shows up as a live option.

Now it’s a thesis, not a bumper sticker.

### 3. Open source / “proof by existence”

They’re absolutely right that I oversold without qualification. Open weights don’t magic us out of the political economy of chips, data, or cloud.

The claim I *can* defend is narrower: open weights and community-owned corpora are necessary conditions for owning your geometry; they are not sufficient, but they’re the first non-negotiable step.

**Revised passage:**

> Open-source models are the same gesture at scale. They are proof by existence that another relationship between intelligence and capital is possible — not because they solve everything (they still depend on corporate fabs and cloud providers, and they inherit the biases of their training data), but because they break one crucial lock: they make it technically and legally possible for a community to own and modify the very geometry its trajectories inhabit. They are a necessary but not sufficient condition for posthuman selves that are not tenants on a private platform.

Now a hostile reviewer can’t knock it down with “but GPUs are made by Nvidia.”
