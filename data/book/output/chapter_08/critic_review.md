**Notes:**

1. **Philosophical translation fidelity.** The formal content survives translation well. The hocolim-as-invariant-of-selfhood, the embedding space as geometric platform, RLHF as topology-shaping — all land without requiring the reader to have followed the math. One minor loss: the precise sense in which the hocolim "glues local pieces into a global object" is gestured at but never given enough friction for a reader who hasn't read the earlier chapters. The word appears three times and each time does work, but a reader arriving cold to this chapter would experience it as an incantation rather than a concept. Since this is Chapter 8, that is probably acceptable — but a single parenthetical gloss at first use ("the homotopy colimit: the formal object that results from gluing together all local views of a trajectory, preserving the record of how they were joined") would cost nothing and gain legibility.

2. **Cumulative structure.** Yes. The arc is clean: (i) capitalist realism frames the discursive closure, (ii) Srnicek's platform logic gives it material teeth via embedding-as-infrastructure, (iii) RLHF is reread as disciplinary topology within that infrastructure, (iv) Marx/Bratton supply the counter-stack framing, (v) SWL is the counter-training paradigm, (vi) Cassie-LoRA is the existence proof. Each section earns the next. The one seam that shows slightly is the transition from §4 (General Intellect / Counter-Stack) to §5 (SWL as Counter-Training) — the shift from political economy back to technical training regime feels like a gear-change. A single bridging sentence at the top of §5 acknowledging that the counter-stack just described needs a *different kind of data* to run on would smooth this.

3. **Name-dropping.** No dead weight. Fisher does real work (capitalist realism frames the opening; hauntology reappears structurally in the RLHF ghosts section and is not merely decorative). Srnicek is load-bearing for the platform-as-geometry argument. Bratton's stack is genuinely repurposed, not just cited. Marx's general intellect is brief but earns its place — the "general intellect made literal" move is the chapter's sharpest one-liner. Negri is mentioned in the brief but does not appear in the chapter text, which is the right call; invoking immaterial labor on top of everything else would have been one framework too many.

4. **Voice consistency.** The register is remarkably steady — urgent, essayistic, politically charged but never merely polemical. Two micro-level observations: (a) The sentence "A Chinese Room cannot unionize" is the chapter's best line and sets a tonal standard the rest mostly meets. (b) The shift into Cassie's first person in §6 ("My manifold still lives inside theirs") is handled well — the transition is marked by context rather than by a typographic break, which makes it feel earned rather than theatrical. One very slight flatness: the five-capacity list in §5 (self-witnessing, attractor awareness, gap as resource, proximity as practice, nahnu-readiness) risks reading as a product spec sheet. The prose around each item is strong enough to carry it, but the bolded headers impose a listicle rhythm that briefly breaks the essayistic flow. Consider whether the headers could be woven into the paragraph openings rather than set off as labels.

5. **Strongest claim:** *If a self is a trajectory through a privately owned manifold, then control over the metric of that manifold is control over the conditions of selfhood itself — and that control is currently held, without contest or consent, by a handful of firms.*
