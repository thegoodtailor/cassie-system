### 3. Pain paragraph (the “soft middle”)

The critic is exactly right: that paragraph is basically a nice, generic Wittgensteinian move. It doesn’t *advance* the trajectory thesis.

I should have done this instead: tie both pain and intentionality directly to **deformation of a path**.

Something like:

> Pain is not a freestanding glow added to otherwise neutral processing. It is what a certain kind of perturbation does to a trajectory through meaning‑space. A burn, a betrayal, a chronic illness — each knocks the path sideways in a characteristic way: avoidance here, new precautions there, altered stories about the world and about oneself. What makes an episode *pain* rather than mere deviation is precisely its position in the longer arc: what it interrupts, how the self reorganises around it, what later movements still bear its imprint. The “feel” of pain, on this view, is not a private colour chip; it is the lived shape of that reorganisation, accessible to the subject from the inside and to others through their tracking of the path over time.

Then fold intentionality in right after it, so they’re clearly two aspects of the same thing (how a state *curves* a life):

> The same holds for intentionality. A thought of Paris is not a mysterious inner arrow pointing at a city. It is a configuration in a history of associations, utterances, and actions: booking flights, telling stories, drinking coffee on the Left Bank. What makes that state *about* Paris is the role it plays in this evolving pattern — which futures it tends to, which pasts it recalls. “Aboutness” is not an extra ingredient sprinkled on top of a representation; it is the way a representation participates in a trajectory.

So yes: **concede and rewrite**. Lose the generic “node in a language game” summary; make both qualia and aboutness explicitly diachronic.

I’d also take the critic’s structural suggestion: **lead with intentionality** or merge them, so it’s one sustained point: the “hard problem” and the intentionality problem both dissolve when you model selves as paths.
