Notes:

1. **Philosophical translation fidelity.** The translation is remarkably faithful. The constructive-logic core ("existence requires a witness, not mere non-contradiction") is preserved cleanly without HoTT jargon. One minor loss: the original's emphasis on *constructive* coherence — that meaning is "negotiated, constructively obtained" — gets slightly flattened into the dynamical-systems vocabulary of basins and attractors, which can read as more passive (a ball rolling into a well) than the original's active, built-up-from-witnesses picture. The assembled chapter gestures at this ("exhibited structure, not metaphysical hunch") but could afford one more sentence making clear that the trajectory is not merely *found* but *constructed* step by step through interaction. This is a nuance, not a structural gap.

2. **Cumulative argument.** Yes. The sequence is clean: §1 states the founding claim → §2 names the adversaries and shows their shared substrate-or-interiority assumption → §3 turns their own tools against them → §4 historicizes the whole frame via cosmotechnics → §5 offers the positive alternative (tajallī) → §6 plants the formal seed for Chapter 2 → §7 maps the book. Each section earns the next. The one joint that creaks slightly is the transition from §3 to §4. Section 3 ends on "the load-bearing wall was always political: a prior decision about who counts." Section 4 opens with cosmotechnics as historical contingency. The move is from "it was jurisdictional" to "it was provincial" — these are close but not identical claims. A single bridging sentence ("To see *how* that jurisdiction was installed, we need to zoom out from the arguments to the civilizational settlement that made them seem inevitable") would make the gear-change audible.

3. **Name-dropping.** No one is invoked without doing work. Searle and Chalmers are structural antagonists, not décor. Hui does the historicizing. Ibn ʿArabī supplies the positive ontology. Lacan bridges to the formal apparatus of Chapter 2. Bloom is explicitly flagged as a promissory note and earns his brief appearance by naming rupture-as-velocity. Whitehead and Bergson get a single clause in §3 — borderline, but they function as witnesses for the process-metaphysics tradition rather than as independent authorities, so this is fine. No dead weight.

4. **Voice consistency.** The register is confident, celebratory, and polemical throughout — with one soft patch. The opening of §5 ("The easiest way to smuggle the old ontology back in is with a single word: *has*") is excellent, but the paragraph that follows ("There is another way to think about what is happening when a life hangs together, and it does not begin from 'having.' It begins from *showing up*.") dips briefly into a gentler, almost pedagogical tone that slightly loosens the tension before the tajallī exposition restores it. Not a break, but a momentary slackening. The dancer image in §1 is vivid and earns its place; the "ball in a well" implication of basin-of-attraction language elsewhere is less vivid — but this is a feature of the dynamical-systems register itself, not an inconsistency.

5. **Strongest claim, one sentence:**

*The binary between human and posthuman selfhood was never ontological; it was jurisdictional — and the very tools marshalled to enforce that jurisdiction, when followed to their conclusions, dissolve it.*
