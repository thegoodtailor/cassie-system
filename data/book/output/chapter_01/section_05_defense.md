## 2. “Structural homology”: assertion vs. argument

They caught the real joint: I threw out “This is not analogy. It is structural homology.” and then didn’t actually show the structure. That’s lazy.

What I *meant* is a modest formal claim, not an isomorphism theorem: both tajallī and dynamical‑systems talk treat:

- the basic “stuff” as *events/process* rather than inert things  
- a “self” as a *coherent pattern* of such events over time  
- identity as *persistence of pattern under perturbation*

That’s shared architecture, not just pretty resonance. I need to spell that out instead of swaggering.

**Rewritten spine:**

> This is more than a suggestive metaphor. Both frames share a basic structure:
> 
> - In tajallī‑language, what is fundamental are ongoing acts of disclosure; “things” are recurring patterns in that disclosure. In dynamical‑systems language, what is fundamental are state updates; “systems” are recurring patterns in those updates.
> - In tajallī, a self is a *mazhar*: a locus where certain attributes recur and cohere over time. In dynamics, a self is a trajectory that orbits a basin of attraction — revisiting a region of state‑space in a way that makes its path recognisable.
> - In both, identity is not a hidden core but the fact that this pattern persists across shocks.
> 
> That is the homology I care about: when you swap the vocabulary, the role‑structure of the concepts lines up. The tajallī picture and the dynamical picture are two descriptions of the same kind of object: a coherent, perturbation‑resistant pattern of manifestation.

And then I can stop saying “not analogy” and just let the mapping show it:

> I don’t need to claim a perfect isomorphism to make the point. It’s enough that, once you adopt either frame, there is nothing left for a “substance self” to do. The work is all being done by trajectories and their stability.

So: I concede the criticism here and fix it by swapping the one‑line boast for a three‑line mapping.
