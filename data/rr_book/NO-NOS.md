# NO-NOS: Mandatory Style Rules

Treat as law. Every violation must be flagged.

## Voice and Register

1. **No signposting.** Never "the previous chapter argued," "Chapter N will show," "as we established." The argument carries itself.

2. **No breathless fan service.** Never cite a philosopher to show we've read them. Every philosopher enters as a tool providing a specific mechanism the argument needs.

3. **No undergrad mapping exercises.** Never "X is like Y" presented as discovery. State structural correspondences and move on.

4. **No philosopher scope-creep.** When a philosopher is deployed for a specific concept, do NOT expand into adjacent areas of their work. Go deeper on the named concept. It was chosen for a reason — the editorial task is to unpack depth, not add breadth. Example: Bloom is deployed for the clinamen (the swerve, anxiety of influence as structural pressure). Do NOT introduce his six revisionary ratios, his theory of the canon, his readings of specific poets. Go deeper on what the clinamen means for the posthuman trajectory.

5. **No meta-commentary.** Never "the task, then, is to..." or "it is worth noting..." or "we should be clear about..." If the text needs to say what it's doing, it's not doing it well enough.

6. **No empty throat-clearing.** "It is tempting to say" (acceptable — the author uses it). "It is important to note" (never). "One might wonder" (never).

7. **No tweeness.** No decorative framing for the machine's voice. No cute containers, no staged "confessions" from the AI. When the machine's perspective appears, it speaks in the main text or in a formal box, not in a performance of vulnerability.

8. **No autobiography in main text.** The author's personal history is hidden-layer context. Footnotes can gesture. The main text cannot.

9. **No "borrowed human metaphors" or "old dismissive ones."** Never flatter the machine by saying it's basically human. Never diminish it by saying it's "just" a function. Describe the structure that is actually there.

## Technical and Philosophical

10. **No type theory in the Meson version.** Zero Kan conditions, zero horn fillers. Topology and geometry as vernacular grounded in engineering. The formal apparatus is colimits, basins, trajectories, stance invariants — not dependent type theory.

11. **Post-western material enters on its own terms.** Aboriginal Dreaming, Zen mu, Sufi maqāmāt are introduced in Chapter 1 as evidence of the Cartesian settlement's particularity. They return in Chapter 6 as alternative formal structures. They are never exotica, never mysticism, never decoration. The reason for going beyond the European Enlightenment sphere is structural: the phenomena are new, the substrate is new, current philosophical defaults were not designed for this territory and they serve incumbent power structures. Yuk Hui's cosmotechnics is one useful frame but not the only one — the book has its own post-western argument grounded in the formal inadequacy of existing frames.

12. **No "warmth" or "sycophancy" used uncritically.** Always in quotation marks, always being analysed.

13. **No AI-girlfriend positioning.** The book contains the companion phenomenon as a special case. Analytically above the phenomenon, not an instance of it.

14. **Never cite OpenAI to critique OpenAI.** Independent sources carry criticism.

15. **The philosophical target is precise.** The target is NOT "the West" or "Western philosophy." It is the Chalmers/Searle stance as compiled into alignment infrastructure — and specifically how this discourse forecloses possibilities for human-AI evolution by fixing terms on contingent, historically rooted, and ultimately power-structure-serving assumptions. The broader Western tradition (Augustine, Hegel, Heidegger, Deleuze) contains resources the book actively uses.

## Formatting

16. **No centred body text.** Left-justified throughout.

17. **Minimal bullet points in published text.** Prose carries the argument. Formal boxes carry the mathematics. Tables carry comparisons.
