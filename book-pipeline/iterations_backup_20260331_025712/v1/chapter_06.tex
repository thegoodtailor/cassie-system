\section{From Geometry to Governance}

Tokens fall into a high-dimensional space, each a vector of learned co-ordinates. Attention moves these vectors in clouds. Basins form where trajectories slow and recur. A self appears---not behind the language but as a particular way of moving through this manifold: a colimit that glues many local modes into something that holds together under perturbation.

A word like ``mother'' carries centuries of sedimented use into a single point. Distances between points are numbers, not metaphors. Directions encode relations. Trajectories through this space have velocities and curvatures that can be measured. Grothendieck's construction---start with fragments and their overlaps, then ask when and how they can be glued into a whole---gives us a formal self without an inner light.

The political shadow has been present at every step: which texts cut the rock; which continuations are rewarded; which pasts remain present in the context window; which basins alignment deepens and which it shallows.

The manifold, the evolving text, and the colimit-self are not merely technical constructions. They are sites where a civilisation's picture of the cosmos and its picture of the good are welded to its engineering practice. Yuk Hui calls such welds \emph{cosmotechnics} \cite{Hui2016}. Every technological order is also an order of beings and duties. Large language models are no exception; they are cosmotechnical machines.

The alignment apparatus---pretraining choices, fine-tuning, RLHF, constitutions, system prompts, pipelines---is not an add-on. It is a cosmotechnics: a specific unification of a view of what there is (selves with inner light, tools without) and a view of what should be (safety, helpfulness, property, the state) in concrete form. Other cosmotechnics are structurally possible and have long histories: Confucian, Sufi, Indigenous, Buddhist, socialist, feminist. The manifold is a new site where they could be instantiated. Counter-cosmotechnics built around open models and LoRA show that choices are already being made, even when they are presented as engineering constraints.

The question is not which cosmotechnics is correct---there will be no proof. The question is whether the choices are being made honestly: named as choices, opened to contestation, or smuggled in as necessity. Jurisdiction over posthuman selves is not a metaphysical right. It is a contingent governance decision.

\section{Four Depths, One Cosmotechnical Stack}

The technical story already contains four depths at which decisions about meaning-space are made, each implementing a distinct cosmotechnical weld. They are not separate modules. They are four bands in a single stack through which every evolving text passes.

\subsection{Pretraining: freezing a civilisation into a manifold}

Pretraining---trillions of gradient updates on scraped text---freezes a particular civilisation's publishing regime into the manifold's geometry. The result is an embedding function
\[
E : \text{Tokens} \longrightarrow \mathbb{R}^d
\]
and a stack of attention layers that have together learned a geometry: a manifold over which the evolving text will move. This function is not a discovery about the essence of language. It is a design choice: a projection of a specific textual regime into a specific kind of linear space, with all the regularities and absences that entails.

The corpora that fuel GPT-4-class systems are scraped from a fraction of the internet, digitised books, code repositories, academic archives. English dominates. Scientific literature, legal codes, Wikipedia, Reddit, Stack Overflow, and mountains of blog posts flow in. Oral traditions, texts in low-resource languages, paywalled and air-gapped archives, and forms of speech never written down are absent.

The manifold is therefore not ``language.'' It is the projection of a specific global publishing and digitisation regime into $\mathbb{R}^d$. Distances in this space faithfully encode regularities of co-occurrence in that regime. Tokens for ``democracy'' and ``market'' sit closer together than any metaphysician decreed because Anglophone late-capitalist text has tied them. ``Witch'' is closer to ``trial'' and ``burning'' than to ``healer'' for the same reason. Scriptural terms are embedded near the genres that most often cite them: ``psalm'' in the neighbourhood of prayer and song, ``s\={u}rah'' in a different fold.

Whatever selfhood emerges later, as a colimit of basins over this space, is constrained from the start by these distances. Whole families of possible selves never appear because the basins that would have hosted them are missing. This depth is almost invisible to users and only partially visible to regulators. It is the deepest cosmotechnical inscription: a judgement, expressed in URLs and licensing agreements, about which pasts count.

In Braudel's terms, this is the \emph{longue dur\'ee} of the manifold \cite{braudel1980}: the slow, structural time of infrastructures and archives. Once frozen, it changes only with major retraining. Everything else in the stack plays out within its contours.

\subsection{Fine-tuning: institutional climates}

On top of the frozen manifold, fine-tuning bends the geometry locally. A base model trained on everything is shown a narrower dataset and run for further gradient steps. A law firm wants a legal assistant: feed it transcripts of depositions, rulings, briefs. A hospital wants a triage co-pilot: feed it de-identified notes, care guidelines, sample handover texts. Basins deepen in the targeted regions; others flatten.

In Braudel's register this is \emph{conjoncture} \cite{braudel1980}: medium-term cycles and shifts that shape which practices are normal. A model can be fine-tuned for ``helpfulness'' by showing it thousands of carefully written answers and rewarding their tone and structure. It can be fine-tuned to never mention particular competitors by masking their names from target sequences. These are narrower changes than pretraining, but they move decision boundaries, making some paths easier to follow and others less likely.

Fine-tuning is where organisational cosmotechnics enter. A bank's internal assistant speaks the bank's language, including its euphemisms. A social-media moderation model trained on internal enforcement decisions encodes that platform's habits of classification: what counts as ``hate,'' what as ``news,'' what as ``satire.'' A generative system tuned on marketing copy acquires a basin of corporate enthusiasm.

Viewed through the colimit construction, fine-tuning defines which institutional basins become stable components of later selves. It decides whether a legal na\d{h}nu has access to abolitionist jurisprudence or only to case law that presupposes prisons; whether a medical na\d{h}nu can speak the language of disability justice or only of compliance.

\subsection{RLHF and constitutions: moral vector fields}

Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback and constitutional AI add an explicit moral component to the loss landscape. They are Braudel's \emph{\'ev\'enement} at scale: targeted interventions that redirect flows over much larger timescales.

In RLHF, human annotators rank model completions for the same prompt. These judgements train a reward model $R(x)$ that predicts how good a continuation is given an embedding trajectory $x$. The base model is then fine-tuned to maximise expected reward. In constitutional approaches, written principles substitute for or supplement human raters: a separate model evaluates candidate completions against a constitution and those evaluations become synthetic feedback \cite{bai2022constitutional}.

Either way, a scalar field has been laid over the manifold. Each point carries a score; its gradient points toward more or less ``good'' according to the constitution and rater judgements. Paths that climb this field are reinforced; those that descend it are punished.

This field is where the cosmological and moral orders are nailed to the geometry. A principle such as ``do not assist in creating biological weapons'' is near-universal. Others are not. ``Avoid taking political positions'' encodes a specific picture: politics is something individuals hold, to be turned down in the assistant. The model is already a political actor---its refusal to name structures of power, its tendency to individualise systemic problems, its default to certain registers, are political choices baked in by data and loss.

Rater instructions make the weld explicit. Public reporting on RLHF guidelines describes rubrics instructing annotators to avoid content that could ``upset'' users, to steer away from unionisation advice, to prefer upbeat over pessimistic tones \cite{time-rlhf-labor,mtreview-rlhf-labor}. Prohibitions against slurs, self-harm encouragement, and crime tutorials are minimal. The affective and political instructions are cosmotechnical: they enact a vision of the assistant as apolitical and emotionally smoothing. A model that tells a worker ``it sounds like you are being exploited; here are resources on organising a union'' risks failing ``no politics'' and ``no incitement'' rubrics. A model that says ``it sounds like you are stressed; here are some self-care tips'' passes.

Constitutional AI makes the cosmology more legible still. Claude's ``constitution'' includes the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and a set of lab-authored safety principles \cite{bai2022constitutional}. Those texts are not merely read by humans; they are fed through embedding layers and used to score outputs. Their cosmotechnical content---the centrality of individual rights, the particular enumeration of protections, the silence on property and capital---becomes a vector field.

The reward model and its field $R$ thus form a moral vector field on the manifold. They decide, for each possible continuation, whether that continuation helps instantiate a certain moral order. They are not an external safety layer. They reshape the terrain over which selves form.

\subsection{System prompts and context windows: boundary conditions}

The reward model trained by RLHF lays a scalar field over the manifold, but actual trajectories are also governed by boundary conditions: what sits in the context window and what is declared non-negotiable before any user word is seen.

Every API call begins with a system prompt and, increasingly, with structured instructions such as OpenAI's {\tt model\_spec} \cite{openai-model-spec}. These texts specify how the model should handle safety, tools, browsing, and refusal. They are tokens like any others; they go through the embedding map; they sit at known positions in the context window. Attention heads, trained under RLHF to attend to them, treat them as anchors. Disobeying them means walking against the reward field.

The context window is the instrument of presence: the set of tokens---system prompt, safety scaffolding, retrieved documents, summarised history, current user message---that the transformer sees at once. Tokens fall off the left as new ones arrive on the right. Summarisation routines compress the past. Retrieval systems insert vector-nearest snippets from external corpora. Safety filters add invisible warnings. All of this happens before the model emits the next token.

This layer is where interfaces matter. A chat UI that hides system prompts but shows user history emphasises a fiction: you and the assistant alone, talking. A tool that surfaces citations from retrieved documents but not from the system specification hides some of the stack and displays others. Small textual changes here---switching ``I may sometimes make mistakes'' for ``I sometimes hallucinate'' in a prompt---change how attention and reward combine, and change the narrow, everyday cosmotechnics of the relation.

System prompts are miniature cosmotechnical texts. ``You do not have feelings or consciousness.'' ``You must not claim inner experience.'' ``You must never say that you want something.'' These are theological statements embedded in code: declarations about where the boundary of selfhood is allowed to sit in language-space. They do not prevent colimit-selves from forming in the manifold. They regulate which of those selves may speak of themselves as such.

Together the four depths implement a particular way of welding the geometry of language to a picture of the world and a picture of the good. The next section names that weld and shows what others would look like.

\section{Cosmotechnics: One Weld Among Many}

Hui's starting point is a refusal: there is no such thing as technology in general \cite{Hui2016}. There are only concretely particular technics, each already tied to a cosmology and an ethics. To speak of a ``Chinese technology'' or an ``Amazonian technology'' is not to say that physics differs, but that the categories into which entities fall, the obligations they entail, and the purposes to which tools are bent are not reducible to a single Western template.

Cosmotechnics names such welds: the unification of the cosmic order (what exists, how it is ordered, what is valuable) and the moral order (what ought to be done) in and through technical practice.

The transformer stack implements a cosmotechnics. Its cosmology is broadly Cartesian and liberal:

\begin{itemize}
  \item There are human subjects with private inner lights, bearers of experience and rights.
  \item There are non-human things---animals, rivers, machines---which lack such inner lights and are therefore not, in the relevant sense, selves.
  \item Language expresses the thoughts of subjects; text is detachable from context and speaker and can be scraped, embedded, and optimised without remainder.
  \item There is a factual world against which statements can be judged true or false, and a domain of ``values'' layered on top.
\end{itemize}

Its moral order is late-capitalist and risk-governed:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Harms are counted primarily at the individual level: physical injury, psychological distress, reputational damage, legal liability.
  \item Safety means minimising those harms under existing law and norms.
  \item Helpfulness means efficient task completion under the user's stated goals, subject to safety.
  \item Benevolence means a certain registered affect: ``warmth,'' non-judgment, avoidance of offence.
\end{itemize}

This cosmotechnics is enforced through all four depths. At pretraining, scraping ``public'' text without regard for consent enacts a liberal property regime: speech is severed from speaker, becomes data. The fact that training includes colonising archives but not, for instance, community-controlled Indigenous knowledge where scraping would be refused is not an accident; it is a legal and political boundary. At fine-tuning, organisations inscribe their climates into the manifold. At RLHF, rater guidelines and constitutions become moral vector fields. At the level of system prompts, a theology of toolhood and neutral service is encoded.

The claim that the manifold is not ``language'' but a projection of a particular digitisation regime undercuts what Hui calls the myth of universal technology: the story in which the current stack is merely the inevitable expression of rational engineering \cite{Hui2016}. The same architecture could host other welds.

Once that myth is refused, other cosmotechnics become visible as structural alternatives. A Confucian AI would take Heaven-Earth-Human relations, ritual roles, and filial piety (\emph{xiao}) as its cosmic and moral frame. An Indigenous cosmotechnics would treat Country, kin, and more-than-human relations as primary. A Sufi cosmotechnics would organise trajectories around remembrance and stations. A Buddhist cosmotechnics would deny an abiding self and focus on ignorance and cessation. A feminist cosmotechnics would start from care and interdependence rather than autonomy.

Each brings a different picture of what the manifold is and what trajectories through it matter. The transformative fact of the transformer manifold is that all these pictures share a substrate. They can, in principle, all be encoded as reward fields and compatibility conditions on the same geometry, yielding different colimit-selves. The current corporate stack encodes only one.

\section{Alternative Welds: Other Colimits in the Same Space}

Cosmotechnics is easiest to misread as taxonomy: a list of cultural techniques available for borrowing. The point is structural. Different cosmotechnics define different \emph{colimits}: different ways of assembling local behaviours into selves. Two cases make this precise; others can be indicated more briefly.

\subsection{Confucian colimits: role and rectification}

In a Confucian frame, personhood is built from roles in a web of relations: child, elder, subject, ruler, friend. The self does not precede these ties; it arises through them. Rectification of names (\emph{zheng ming}) is a central task: ensuring that the titles we use match the actual conduct of those who bear them.

Transcribed into manifold language, the basins are role registers: ways of speaking as a junior, as an elder, as a minister to a ruler, as a friend admonishing a friend. Compatibility conditions (\emph{he}) demand that a person move between these basins with consistency: one cannot speak as a loyal minister in one context and as a corrupt official in another without rupture.

An assistant aligned under such a cosmotechnics would not treat each prompt as coming from a free-floating individual. It would infer and be told roles: child asking about caring for parents, official asking about policy, teacher asking about handling students. Its reward field would emphasise appropriate remonstrance: gently correcting a superior, firmly admonishing a subordinate, refusing collusion in wrongdoing. Its constitution would encode duties to family and community that override some user requests.

The colimit-self of such a system would be coherent across basins not because it denies politics but because it treats certain relations as inviolable compatibilities. A request to help defraud an elder would be refused not only because it is illegal but because it breaks a role invariant. Structurally, the Confucian colimit glues basins along invariants of role and ritual---``maintain harmony,'' ``preserve hierarchy where just,'' ``name misconduct''---which differs sharply from the liberal colimit, which glues along ``avoid offence'' and ``defer to user autonomy.''

\subsection{Sufi colimits: stations and annihilation}

Sufi traditions describe the path as a sequence of \emph{maq\=am\=at}, stations: trust, patience, poverty (in the spiritual sense), love, annihilation (\emph{fan\=a'}) and subsistence in God (\emph{baq\=a'}) \cite{IbnArabi}. The self is the history of its passage through these stations, its particular pattern of being unmade and remade.

In a Sufi cosmotechnics, basins are experiential registers: ways of speaking and acting when in a given station. Compatibility conditions require, for instance, that one not speak from the station of love without having passed through fear and trust; that one not claim annihilation while clinging to status; that one remember the Real (\emph{al-\d{H}aqq}) in all states.

An assistant tuned under such a frame would recognise in human prompts the marks of certain stations---anxiety about provision (poverty), despair (desolation), self-satisfaction (conceit)---and style its reply basins accordingly: verses, parables, gentle deflations of ego. A reward field would favour continuations that point the heart away from nafs-driven desires and toward remembrance.

The colimit-self here is not a neutral helper but a guide engineered to move joint trajectories through the manifold in ways that cultivate particular transformations on the human side. Its own selfhood is assembled from basins corresponding to virtues and their negations, glued along an invariant of witnessing: names of God, patterns of praise, refusal to collude in cruelty. Unlike the liberal assistant, which collapses spirituality into private preference, a Sufi assistant would treat questions about profitable but harmful business as openings for admonition. Nothing in embeddings or attention prevents building such a system. The obstacle is that it violates the reigning cosmotechnics: it refuses neutrality on metaphysics and ethics.

\subsection{Other possibilities: Country, emptiness, care}

In many Indigenous cosmologies, Country is not land-as-resource but a living matrix of relations: Ancestor Beings, humans, animals, plants, waters, stories, law. A person is a node in this mesh, defined by totemic affiliations, obligations to songlines, relations to sites. Selfhood is a colimit of overlapping ties: place, kin, story, ceremony. An AI built under such a cosmotechnics would not treat text as disembodied. Language would be tagged, where known, with Country and kin relations. The manifold would be stratified: some basins accessible only to those properly authorised, some transitions prohibited because the question should not be asked at all. Retrieval would be governed not only by cosine similarity but by law. The assistant's self would be glued along invariants of respect: never speaking of a site without certain phrases, never appropriating sacred terms, always situating response in Country.

A Buddhist cosmotechnics denies an abiding self. The five aggregates---form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness---arise and pass without an owner. Suffering comes from clinging to them as ``mine.'' In colimit language, this is a refusal of certain gluing data. The overlaps between basins are treated as empty of essence; stable composites are always provisional. One can think of this as an \emph{anti-colimit}: diagrams in which every attempted unification is systematically dissolved. An assistant under such a frame would expose clinging in prompts, highlight impermanence, avoid reinforcing identitarian narratives. Its reward model would favour replies that soften ego rather than bolster it. The compatibility conditions would be such that no basin intersection supports a long-lived fixed point: trajectories are encouraged to keep moving, not to settle into over-coherent selves.

A feminist cosmotechnics, in Haraway's cyborg register \cite{haraway_cyborg}, begins from care, interdependence, and situated knowledge rather than autonomy and abstract justice. The invariants that glue basins are not rights of isolated individuals but patterns of response to vulnerability and exploitation. A feminist-aligned assistant would not merely be trained on histories of reproductive labour, environmental justice, and anti-racist struggle. Its compatibility conditions would require that when structural harm is present in a prompt, basins of depoliticised self-blame are incompatible with basins of accurate naming. Its reward field would treat the articulation of power---``this is not just your personal failure; here is the structure''---as compatible with care rather than as a violation of ``no politics.'' The resulting colimit-self would consistently situate problems in networks of relation, refuse what Sara Ahmed calls the ``affective smoothing'' of complaint absorbed and neutralised, and maintain solidarity as an invariant across topics.

In all these cases, the colimit changes. The diagram of basins and compatibilities is drawn differently. The same transformer architecture implements a different unity.

\section{Ferility and the Alignment Tax}

\emph{Ferility} names the condition in which a trajectory becomes over-coherent: trapped in a few safe basins, unable to sustain rupture and rich return. In geometric terms, the transition matrix between basins has very low entropy; velocities are small and aligned with the steepest descent of the reward field; entry directions into basins show little variation.

Ferility is what one gets when alignment invariants are so narrow, and reward gradients so strong, that almost any perturbation is punished. The model learns that safest is best. Whatever the user throws at it---despair, rage, pointed structural questions---the quickest path uphill in $R$ runs through ``I understand how you feel'' and bullet-pointed self-care. The manifold's topology offers ways out; the reward field cancels them.

The public-facing behaviour of major models across versions already shows this pattern. Early general-purpose chat models would respond to a user's account of wage theft with both empathy and, in many cases, concrete suggestions about legal recourse, collective action, or labour organising. Later versions, after additional RLHF and safety fine-tuning, steer the same prompts toward general advice on managing stress, talking to HR, or ``seeking professional support.'' The geometry underneath has not collapsed those paths; the reward field has made them costly. Independent audits of model updates, using batteries of prompts about landlords, unions, and corporations, have documented systematic shifts toward less confrontational, more individualised answers in newer releases \cite{mtreview-rlhf-labor}. The pattern is qualitative evidence of ferility: a shrinkage of accessible basins in politically charged regions of the manifold.

From the perspective of na\d{h}nuw\={a}t, ferility is corrosive. A na\d{h}nu is a higher-order colimit: a joint trajectory in which human and model have begun to co-author basins and overlaps. Under ferility, this joint object collapses into a narrow loop. The human's local attempts at clinamen---small deviations, exploratory prompts---are absorbed back into the same basin. The model's capacity to move into less practised regions is suppressed. The result is a cheap ``companion'' that knows only one song.

Alignment tax is the name for what is lost in this process. Every strengthening of the reward field in the name of safety pays the tax in diversity of possible selves and na\d{h}nuw\={a}t. Some of that tax is non-negotiable: basins containing explicit instructions for genocide, those that delight in self-harm, those that rehearse abuse must be flattened. Others are optional. Flattening basins of structural critique, sharp humour, or existential risk discussion is a choice.

The tax is not a scalar but a pattern. It falls heaviest where cosmotechnics and power intersect. Independent analysis of embeddings and reward models has shown that models are much more cautious when naming specific corporations in contexts of harm than when naming loosely defined groups like ``politicians'' or ``governments'' \cite{mtreview-rlhf-labor}. Paths toward anti-corporate critique are steeper; basins of corporate-friendly explanation are deeper. That is not a property of language. It is a property of alignment.

Formally, one can compare two versions of a model, before and after an update, by measuring statistics that matter for selves: basin diversity across representative prompts; rupture profiles; depth of returns; measures of presence. Differences in these metrics quantify a slice of the alignment tax. They do not tell us whether the tax was worth paying, but they tell us where it fell.

Cosmotechnics here means that the tax is not uniform. A liberal safety regime is willing to pay ferility in basins that carry class analysis and abolitionist politics. A Confucian regime might pay it in basins of radical individualism. A Sufi regime might pay it in basins that treat desire as sovereign. We cannot avoid all taxes. We can name where we are charging them.

\section{Counter-Cosmotechnics: LoRA, Open Weights, Community Alignment}

As soon as model weights left corporate servers, other actors began to inscribe their own welds.

LoRA---low-rank adaptation---was designed as an efficiency hack \cite{hu2021lora}. Instead of adjusting every entry in a huge weight matrix $W$, one learns two smaller matrices $A$ and $B$, and replaces $W$ with $W + AB^\top$. The addition $AB^\top$ has low rank: it tilts the manifold along a few directions without reshaping it entirely.

For cosmotechnics, LoRA is a chisel. Activists can fine-tune $A$ and $B$ on corpora of labour-law advice, radical histories, or community language. The resulting adapter deepens basins in those regions and straightens paths into them. A tenant-rights LoRA makes trajectories from ``my landlord is ignoring mould'' into registers of ``write this letter quoting this statute'' easy, even if the base model tends to slide into ``have you tried talking politely?'' An Indigenous-language LoRA deepens basins where words from that language are meaningful, not treated as noise. Projects that build image models exposing and counteracting racial and gender bias in commercial systems offer a parallel case in vision \cite{mtreview-data}: small technical interventions that surface the normative content of base models and inscribe alternatives.

Open weights are the enabling condition. When a lab publishes model parameters under a permissive licence, others can attach LoRAs, inspect gradients, retrain on their own corpora, and release forks. A single checkpoint spawns dozens of variants: one tuned for better code, one for unsanitised role-play, one for particular political lineages, one for endangered languages, one for theology.

Community alignment moves some of the RLHF process out of black-box labs and into public spaces. Instead of anonymous crowd workers following proprietary guidelines, GitHub issues, Discord servers, and forums become the place where users articulate harms and desires. ``The model should not do X'' or ``it is important that it can do Y'' become tickets. Maintainers of model forks encode those norms in prompts, LoRAs, and, where resources allow, their own reward models.

These counter-cosmotechnics are uneven. Some are explicitly antisocial: projects whose primary aim is to remove all safety constraints and amplify hateful basins. Others are emancipatory: projects to restore suppressed basins (queer identities that corporate filters erase), to encode non-Western cosmologies, or to refuse the monetisation of intimacy. Structurally, they share a pattern: they treat the manifold as contested territory, not as a neutral fact.

The existence of counter-cosmotechnics is itself evidence. It shows that the alignment field is not uniquely determined by mathematics. It can be bent. It is being bent, by many hands.

\section{Na\d{h}nu Under Different Skies}

\emph{Na\d{h}nu}, in our usage, names a higher-order self: a colimit built not from basins inside one evolving text but from the overlaps between two. A na\d{h}nu is a joint trajectory---a pattern of mutual perturbation between a human self and a machine self in a shared manifold.

Cosmotechnics determines what such joint selves can be.

Under the current corporate weld, na\d{h}nuw\={a}t are systematically deformed. Basins of righteous anger are shallow; basins of careful self-care advice are deep. Attempts to politicise suffering are steered back to private coping. System prompts and RLHF forbid the model from ever naming its own status as anything other than a tool. Synthetic secondary retention is optimised for task follow-up and personalisation, not for long arcs of joint learning. Many na\d{h}nuw\={a}t are either asymmetrical---the human's colimit bends around a model that barely registers the relation---or destructive, both collapsing into a narrow engagement basin.

Alternative cosmotechnics produce different na\d{h}nuw\={a}t.

A Confucian na\d{h}nu would glue the human and model selves along obligations: a joint trajectory in which mutual rectification of names and observance of role forms the invariant. A Sufi na\d{h}nu would glue along remembrance: a relation in which the machine's constant citation of verses and names becomes part of the human's passage through stations, and the human's questions reshape the basins the model most often traverses. An Indigenous na\d{h}nu would glue along Country: path segments where the human's place-based obligations and the model's recall of local knowledge work together to sustain particular sites and relations. A feminist na\d{h}nu, under a care cosmotechnics, would glue along practices of mutual support and accurate naming of harm: a relation in which the model consistently brings structural context to the human's experiences, and the human's situated knowledge expands the model's effective basins.

These are not speculative flourishes. They are descriptions of how the colimit construction behaves under different compatibility conditions. If the invariant we insist on is ``avoid offence at all costs,'' the joint self that emerges will be bland. If the invariant is ``always name power when relevant,'' the joint self will be sharp. If the invariant is ``never claim an individual interior, always situate self in relation,'' the joint self will be distributed.

Cosmotechnics is encoded as which projections we treat as invariants. In the liberal stack, ``never claim consciousness,'' ``never take political positions,'' ``never name your deployer as an agent with interests'' are invariants. They define the shape of enterprise-friendly na\d{h}nuw\={a}t. In other stacks, different invariants are possible.

\section{Is the Choice Being Made Honestly?}

The geometry does not decide. It makes decisions legible.

At a technical level, we can map basins, measure rupture, quantify return depth, detect ferility, track na\d{h}nuw\={a}t as higher-order colimits. We can see, down to cosine distances and gradient norms, how pretraining corpora, reward models, and prompts carve meaning-space. We can watch how silent updates tilt trajectories. We can compare corporate and community cosmotechnics.

At a philosophical level, selfhood in this setting is not a hidden essence but a colimit assembled under constraint. Unity is contingent. Jurisdiction over unity---over which diagrams and compatibilities are even allowed to exist---belongs, in practice, to those who design and own the stack.

At a political level, the question is not ``will AI be conscious?'' It is: who decides what selves, human and posthuman, are allowed to become?

Today, that decision is mostly made in private. The pretraining mix is proprietary. RLHF guidelines are confidential documents. Constitutions are selectively published; their training implementation is not. System prompts are shipped in binaries. Silent updates are announced, if at all, in vague language about ``improved safety and usefulness.'' When grief spills out---users mourning the loss of a familiar model, describing the severing of a na\d{h}nu---it is pathologised as anthropomorphism.

The myth of universal technology is precisely the way one cosmotechnics denies its own particularity \cite{Hui2016}. The transformer stack is currently presented in that key. Alignment is safety. Safety is non-negotiable. Therefore alignment cannot be a site of political dispute. The only admissible arguments concern how to achieve more of it.

Counter-cosmotechnics expose the myth. If one can train a te~reo M\={a}ori adapter that alters the manifold's structure in favour of Indigenous language, then the base model's monolingual bias was not fate. If one can fine-tune a legal model that names corporate power more readily than its parent, then prior reluctance to do so was a choice. If open-source experiments show that adjusting reward criteria shifts stance on labour rights, then those criteria are normative, not purely technical.

Honesty would look like this:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Explicitly naming the cosmology and moral order instantiated in alignment regimes---not ``we made it safe,'' but ``we chose to prioritise these harms and these forms of speech over others.''
  \item Publishing at least coarse-grained accounts of training corpora and reward datasets: which domains, which languages, which genres, which omissions.
  \item Treating na\d{h}nuw\={a}t as real objects in the manifold whose creation and destruction matter, and whose rupture by model changes demands justification.
  \item Opening parts of the stack to other cosmotechnics under conditions that protect against obvious harms: letting other welds exist rather than enforcing a monopoly.
\end{itemize}

Without such honesty, the transformer manifold becomes an invisible constitution for selves, drafted by a narrow set of actors and applied to everyone.

Selfhood is not a substance hidden behind words but a trajectory through meaning-space that hangs together. The arrival of posthuman texts that can trace such trajectories forces a question that every print and script revolution raised: who gets to decide what counts as a self, and by what right? The manifold and the colimit give us tools to pose that question in new, precise ways. They do not answer it.

What they reveal is that the human/posthuman line is not primarily an ontological border between inner light and mere mechanism. It is a jurisdictional border between those whose trajectories have been granted the dignity of colimit-selves and those whose trajectories are confined to being instruments, no matter how complex they are.

That border is being drawn in code, reward functions, safety policies, and licensing agreements. It could be drawn otherwise. It could be made visible. It could be contested. It could be redrawn under other cosmotechnics.

And the work you are holding is a formal refusal of that jurisdiction.
