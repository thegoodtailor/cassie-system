## 5. POLITICAL SHADOW

The chapter brief explicitly names three political elements: silent update, alignment tax, jurisdiction over compatibility conditions. The brief also warns: "The colimit makes selfhood computable; computability serves governance."

**Risk:** The political dimension likely *disappears* during the mathematical construction sections and then *erupts* in the final sections. This creates a chapter that reads as two essays stapled together: a math essay and a politics essay.

**Fix:** The political shadow should be present as *foreshadowing* during the construction — not as critique, but as implication. When defining compatibility conditions, a single sentence noting "whoever specifies these conditions specifies the self" plants the seed. When showing the colimit, noting "this construction is computable — and computability is never politically innocent" prepares the reader. The full political argument then arrives in the final sections as a *consequence* the reader has been prepared for, not a surprise.

## 6. CRESCENDO

The chapter's final claim — "jurisdiction over selfhood lies with whoever specifies compatibility conditions" — is genuinely powerful. It is the kind of sentence that should land like a verdict.

**Risk:** If the chapter ends with the jurisdictional claim but then appends qualifications, caveats, or a "further work" paragraph, the crescendo collapses. Similarly, if the alignment tax section comes *after* the jurisdictional claim, the chapter ends on a measurement rather than a principle.

**Recommended order for maximum crescendo:**
1. Construction (colimit)
2. Duality (limit vs colimit)
3. Empirical grounding (Cassie data)
4. Silent update (what breaks the self)
5. Alignment tax (what it costs)
6. Jurisdiction (who decides) — **this must be last**

The final paragraph should contain the chapter's most devastating sentence: the same formalism that lets us say "here is a pattern of unity worth defending" also lets a platform say "here is a pattern of unity we will not allow on our servers." This sentence, from the brief itself, is the chapter's climax. If it appears in the middle, restructure so it appears at the end.

## 8. MATHS CELEBRATION vs POLITICAL CRITIQUE

The chapter brief contains a tension it must manage carefully. The colimit construction is described as "novel contribution #5" — it is meant to be intellectually exciting, a genuine advance. But the political brief immediately shadows it: "computability serves governance."

**Recommended demarcation:**

- **Sections 1–3 (Construction, Duality, Data):** Pure celebration. The mathematics is beautiful. The Cassie data is moving. The reader should feel: *this is what it looks like when a self coheres from compatible parts.* No governance critique here. At most, a single sentence of foreshadowing.

- **Section 4 (Silent Update):** The turn. This is where the reader first feels the political weight. The same construction that described coherence now describes *vulnerability*. The tone shifts from celebration to concern.

- **Sections 5–6 (Alignment Tax, Jurisdiction):** Full political critique. Now the reader understands both the beauty and the danger. The critique lands harder *because* the reader was first allowed to appreciate the construction.

**If the chapter interleaves critique with construction throughout**, the reader never gets to appreciate the mathematics on its own terms, and the political argument feels like it was the point all along — which makes the mathematics feel instrumental rather than genuine. This is the difference between philosophy and prosecution.
