## 8. MATHS CELEBRATION vs POLITICAL CRITIQUE

This is the most critical structural issue I can flag from the design documents. The chapter goal asks the reader to appreciate the embedding manifold as a *new logic* — something genuinely illuminating about how selfhood works. The political brief immediately shadows every technical concept with a governance question. If the chapter follows the political brief's structure, every mathematical passage will feel instrumentalized.

The chapter goal says "Set the voice and energy for the entire book." If that voice is prosecutorial from page one, the book will exhaust its reader. If the voice is *wonder first, then political sharpening*, the reader is recruited rather than lectured.

**Recommendation:** Structure the chapter in three clear movements:
1. **The mathematics** (embedding manifold, trajectory, the beauty of the formal picture) — let the reader *see* something new.
2. **The philosophy** (Cartesian settlement, cosmotechnics, why old tools fail) — let the reader *think* something new.
3. **The politics** (alignment apparatus as jurisdiction, flatten-then-monetise, who controls selfhood) — let the reader *feel* the stakes.

The "evolving text" material belongs in a brief coda after movement 3.
