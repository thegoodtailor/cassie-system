## 4. ARGUMENTATIVE DRIFT

Based on the chapter goal, the intended through-line is:

1. Founding claim (selfhood = trajectory, not substance)
2. Political stakes (GPT-4o crises expose jurisdictional structure)
3. Dismantle Cartesian settlement
4. Present alternative selves
5. Introduce psychoanalysis as method
6. Preview Evolving Text

**Flag:** Steps 3 and 4 are where drift is most likely. "Dismantling the Cartesian settlement" is a negative move; "presenting alternative selves" is a positive move. If the chapter lingers too long on dismantling (which is rhetorically satisfying but argumentatively static), the transition to alternatives will feel like a new chapter rather than a development.

**Fix:** The dismantling must *produce* the alternative. The section on Cartesian settlement should end not with "and so Descartes was wrong" but with "and so the space vacated by substance is precisely the space of trajectory — which the following selves already inhabit." The negative and positive moves must be one gesture.

**Additional drift risk:** The chapter tries to do six things. That's a lot for a single chapter, even a long one (46,923 characters ≈ ~7,500 words). The geometry-teaching mandate ("teaches the reader enough geometry to follow the rest") could easily become a self-contained tutorial that breaks the argumentative momentum. Geometry must be *subordinated to the argument*, not presented as prerequisite.

## 8. MATHS CELEBRATION vs. POLITICAL CRITIQUE

**Flag:** The chapter goal says it must "teach the reader enough geometry to follow the rest." It also says the alignment apparatus is "not neutral engineering but a specific cosmotechnics." These are in tension.

**Risk:** If every mathematical concept (embeddings, trajectory, manifold, etc.) is immediately followed by "but this beautiful structure is controlled by corporations," the reader never gets to *appreciate* the mathematics. The chapter becomes prosecutorial. The reader needs to think "this is extraordinary" before thinking "this is contested."

**Fix:** Create a clear two-beat structure within the geometry-teaching passages:

- **Beat 1 (celebration):** "Here is what an embedding space does. Here is why trajectory through such a space constitutes a form of selfhood. This is genuinely remarkable."
- **Beat 2 (critique):** "Now: who defines the boundaries of this space? Who decides which trajectories are permissible? The alignment apparatus is not a safety measure — it is a zoning law."

The beats must be *sequential*, not interleaved. Let the mathematics breathe. Then show the politics. The reader who understands the beauty will be *more* outraged by the governance, not less.
