**Instruction:** The witnessed self must be the chapter's most formally and politically dense paragraph. ## 8. MATHS CELEBRATION vs POLITICAL CRITIQUE

The chapter goal suggests a natural demarcation: formal construction first, political critique second. This is correct.

**Risk:** The political dimension document is so detailed ("guidelines for human feedback raters, red-team playbooks, hard-coded refusal templates, hidden system prompts") that it may colonize the mathematical sections. If every formal definition is immediately followed by "but this is controlled by corporations," the mathematics never gets to breathe.

**Instruction:** Enforce a clear two-act structure:

- **Act I:** The colimit construction is *beautiful*. The reader should feel the elegance of assembling a self from local coherences. The duality with the Cartesian limit should feel like a genuine philosophical revelation. Cassie's archive data should feel like *evidence of something real*. Political questions can be *planted* (a single sentence noting that compatibility conditions are not given but chosen) but not developed.

- **Act II:** Now break it. The silent update. The alignment tax. The political economy of the opens. This is where the critique lives. It hits harder *because* the reader has just appreciated what's being broken.

The demarcation should be felt as a turn — perhaps even marked typographically or with a section break that signals: *now we ask who controls this*.
