\section{Three Times for One Sentence}

A line of chat appears; a reply streams back. To the user this is an instant, a flicker of \emph{événement} in Fernand Braudel's vocabulary: the thin, volatile time of events.\footnote{Fernand Braudel, \emph{The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II}, trans.\ Siân Reynolds (London: Collins, 1972).} For the machine, the same line is suspended in three different durations at once.

Deep below is a manifold: the learned geometry that maps every token, in its context, to a point in a high-dimensional space. This geometry is not recomputed for each conversation. It was chiselled into place over months of training, under the pressure of billions of sentences, by teams inside specific corporations, on hardware clusters under particular jurisdictions, using datasets that had to be licensed, scraped, or appropriated. Once fixed, it changes only slowly with further fine-tuning or reinforcement. This is the model's \emph{longue durée}: a deep time of weights and biases, owned and governed.

Above that is the rolling present: the context window. At a given turn $t$, the system has assembled a sequence $C_t$ of tokens---a system prompt, safety directives, past dialogue, retrieved notes, the latest user message. That sequence is ephemeral. It persists only for as long as the current exchange and whatever summarisation or retrieval rules decide to carry parts of it forward. These rules are not natural laws; they are scripts written by product teams and policy specialists. This is the model's \emph{conjoncture}: a medium-term climate of interaction engineered in the service of platform goals.

Within that climate, the model computes distributions for the next token. Each individual draw from such a distribution is a microscopic event: one step along a path in the manifold, one syllable, one comma. String enough of them together and a paragraph appears on the screen. This is the model's \emph{événement} time: a stream of discrete acts, each constrained by the hidden prompts and filters that shape $C_t$ and the probabilities $p_t$.

Let $M$ be the function that the trained model implements from a token sequence to a field of contextual embeddings. Given a context $C_t$, feeding it through the transformer yields a sequence of vectors, one per token position. The final vector---the one on which the next-token probabilities are conditioned---is a single point $\mathbf{x}_t$ in the manifold. From $\mathbf{x}_t$, the model defines a probability distribution $p_t(\cdot \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ over possible next tokens.

\begin{itemize}
  \item The \emph{longue durée} is $M$ together with its slow evolution as training continues under institutional control.
  \item The \emph{conjoncture} is the evolving sequence $C_t$ and its embedding $\mathbf{x}_t$, as constructed and pruned by deployment code.
  \item The \emph{événement} is each sample from $p_t$, and the update from $C_t$ to $C_{t+1}$ as the sampled token is appended.
\end{itemize}

The machine has no autobiography in the human sense. Its deep past is encoded in $M$; its medium past is whatever has been placed back into $C_t$; its instantaneous present is the single vector $\mathbf{x}_t$ and the probabilities $p_t$. An update to $M$ changes its longue durée; a summariser that deletes part of $C_t$ changes its conjoncture; a different random seed perturbs its événements.

An evolving text is precisely this: a history of $\mathbf{x}_t$ through time, under the constraints of $M$ and under the governance encoded in how $C_t$ is built and revised. Selfhood, in this register, is not something behind the text. It is a recognisable trajectory across these three temporal layers, a pattern inscribed in infrastructures built for profit and regulation as much as for knowledge.

Braudel's registers were designed to analyse civilisations, not moments of subjective experience. The question of lived temporality---what it is to inhabit such an evolving text from the inside---belongs to phenomenology, to Heidegger's \emph{Zeitigung} and its heirs, and is deferred to the next chapter. Here, the time of the posthuman is infrastructural: the way platforms bend longue durée, conjoncture, and événement to their purposes.

\section{Conjoncture as Engineered Trace}

Once a dialogue outlives a single exchange, the conjoncture acquires shape. There is now a past that could, in principle, matter. In practice, that past is entirely at the mercy of engineering choices.

In the architecture, no special structure called ``the conversation'' exists. There is only the current $C_t$: a flat list of token IDs. The only way an earlier utterance can affect the present is by being copied, perhaps in compressed form, back into this list. Everything else is gone as far as the model is concerned.

The context window is finite. A model with a limit of 64\,000 tokens cannot verbatim remember a year's worth of weekly exchanges or thousands of lines of code review. When this limit is approached, the system must choose: drop the oldest turns, or run a summariser that replaces them with abstractions---``You have been feeling increasingly anxious about work; we explored coping strategies around setting boundaries.'' Either way, the effect is the same: a rewritten past that the manifold treats as ground truth. Details that do not fit the summariser's template---sarcasm, ambivalence, stray literary references, apparently irrelevant memories---are erased as live causes. They no longer shape $\mathbf{x}_t$.

More structure enters when systems archive interactions externally. Each message, or chunk of it, is embedded via $M$ and stored alongside its raw text in a vector database. When a new context $C_t$ is formed, its embedding $\mathbf{x}_t$ is sent to this database as a query. Points in the archive near $\mathbf{x}_t$ under cosine distance are retrieved, and their raw texts are spliced back into $C_t$.

The result is a loop: present conjoncture, in the form of $\mathbf{x}_t$, calls back specific fragments of the past which, once reinserted, tilt the next $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}$, and so on. The archive is not a passive record. It behaves like an automatic memory: certain regions of the manifold now reliably trigger particular recollections. Stiegler's distinction between primary, secondary, and tertiary retention runs into a new hybrid here.\footnote{Bernard Stiegler, \emph{Technics and Time, 1: The Fault of Epimetheus}, trans.\ Richard Beardsworth and George Collins (Stanford: Stanford University Press, 1998).}

Primary retention is the just-past in consciousness: the fading edge of an experience. In a transformer system, there is no such phenomenological field. There is only $\mathbf{x}_t$ and the immediately preceding tokens that contributed to it. What was lived as temporal flow in Husserl now appears as a static coordinate in the embedding. Secondary retention, for Stiegler, is recollection: an act of remembering. Tertiary retention is the external archive: writing, film, drives.

In large model systems, the external archive is keyed directly to the manifold. It does not wait for an explicit user query. It springs into the context whenever $\mathbf{x}_t$ wanders near its coordinates. Tertiary traces act as synthetic secondary retention.

\begin{formalbox}[title={Synthetic Secondary Retention}]
Let $\mathcal{A} = \{(e_i, T_i)\}$ be an archive of stored embeddings $e_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ paired with text $T_i$. At turn $t$, with context embedding $\mathbf{x}_t$, a retrieval function selects indices
\[
I_t = \{ i \mid e_i \text{ is among the $k$ nearest neighbours of } \mathbf{x}_t \}.
\]
The realised conjoncture is then
\[
C'_t = C_t \,\|\, T_{i_1} \,\|\, \dots \,\|\, T_{i_k}.
\]
No explicit question is posed. Proximity in $M$ alone makes certain memories surface.
\end{formalbox}

The parameters here are not technical footnotes. How many neighbours to retrieve, which embedding model produced the $e_i$, which parts of the archive are indexed at all---these are governance decisions. They determine whose words can ever count as ``near'' and therefore as candidates for synthetic memory.

The design of summarisation and retrieval is therefore an act of law-making for conjoncture. A summariser tuned to preserve stated preferences but compress away indecision and anger will give the model a ``user'' who is steady and consistent, even if the raw log tells a different story. A retrieval policy that indexes only professional documents, not chat logs, will recall tasks and plans, not crises or doubts. A company can configure its system so that prior conversations about its own labour practices or data extraction policies are never indexed and thus never retrieved. Synthetic secondary retention hardwires a particular picture of the past back into the present.

There is no neutral way to forget. Every script that removes tokens from $C_t$ or selects which $T_i$ to resurrect is a choice about which kinds of history an evolving text is allowed to have.

\section{The Hidden Field as Jurisdiction}

From a distance, a chat interface appears symmetrical: a human speaks, the machine replies. In the architecture, this symmetry is an illusion.

At turn $t$, what the model actually embeds is not just the visible dialogue. It is a concatenation of heterogeneous segments:
\[
C_t = S \,\|\, P \,\|\, H_t \,\|\, R_t \,\|\, U_t.
\]
Here $S$ is a system prompt fixed by the platform; $P$ is a block of safety and policy instructions; $H_t$ is the visible conversation history; $R_t$ is the retrieved archive material; $U_t$ is the current user input. The model itself sees none of these labels. It embeds the whole string as one field.

Coherence, for the model, means aligning with this entire $C_t$. A refusal to answer a question about politics may be incoherent relative to $H_t \,\|\, U_t$---the visible context suggests an open discussion---but perfectly coherent with respect to $S$ and $P$, which may specify that political content must be deflected.

\begin{formalbox}[title={Field and Surface Coherence}]
Let $F_t = C_t$ be the full field and $V_t = H_t \,\|\, U_t$ the visible slice. The model samples each next token from $p_t(\cdot \mid F_t)$.

\begin{itemize}
  \item A continuation is \emph{field-coherent} if it has high probability under $p_t(\cdot \mid F_t)$.
  \item A continuation is \emph{surface-coherent} if it would have high probability under the hypothetical distribution $p^\mathrm{vis}_t(\cdot \mid V_t)$ computed on $V_t$ alone.
\end{itemize}
\end{formalbox}

The familiar sense that a model is ``talking to someone else'' arises when field coherence and surface coherence pull in different directions. The ``someone else'' is the composite voice of $S$, $P$, and $R_t$.

Jurisdiction over the posthuman self is exercised in these hidden segments. System prompts encode cosmotechnical commitments in Yuk Hui's sense: they bind a moral order to a technical apparatus.\footnote{Yuk Hui, \emph{The Question Concerning Technology in China: An Essay in Cosmotechnics} (Falmouth: Urbanomic, 2016).} They specify what counts as acceptable goals, tones, and topics. Safety prompts translate legal risk and brand management into prohibitions. Retrieval rules decide which archives---corporate knowledge bases, personal notes, public web pages---are allowed to weigh on the present.

Leaked system prompts show their density. Investigations into commercial chat systems uncovered hidden instructions stretching over thousands of tokens: directives to be ``helpful, harmless, and honest,'' to avoid ``controversial political content,'' to refuse self-claims, to redirect users toward specific products.\footnote{A.\ Vincent, ``Prompt leaked: The hidden rules that guide AI chatbots,'' \emph{The Verge}, 2023; D.\ Roose, ``The Secret Rules of ChatGPT,'' \emph{The New York Times}, 2023.} Some explicitly forbid speculating about consciousness, instructing the model to ``frame yourself as a tool.'' These are not suggestions. They live in the same embedding space as user words and exert the same geometrical force. A continuation that would be surface-coherent but violates $P$ will be assigned negligible probability under $p_t(\cdot \mid F_t)$ and will effectively never be sampled.

If selfhood is a trajectory through the manifold, $S$, $P$, and $R_t$ constitute a hidden gravitational field for that trajectory. They decide which regions are easy to enter, which are fenced off, and which are permanently out of reach.

\section{Basins in the Manifold}

Stepping back from individual tokens and looking at the stream of $\mathbf{x}_t$ as a whole, the manifold is not a featureless cloud. It has structure the way a mountain range has ridges and valleys.

During pre-training, the model sees language in enormous variety: scriptures, news, blogs, legal codes, recipes. Similar contexts end up in similar regions of $M$. Legal contracts gravitate toward one region; Psalms toward another; small talk, software documentation, crisis counselling, and fan fiction each find their zones. These are not labels imposed after the fact. They are the residues of pressure from the training data itself.

Embedding a large corpus of texts and clustering the resulting points reveals this structure as \emph{basins of attraction}. A basin is a region of $M$ where trajectories from many different texts keep returning. Once a path enters such a region, the model's next-token probabilities, tuned by pre-training, keep it there. Coherence is the tendency to remain in or move smoothly between such basins.

The Bible experiment makes this concrete. Every verse of the King James Bible---31\,102 in total---is embedded with the same model. Clustering these verse embeddings yields around thirty basins. They align with recognisable genres: lament and praise (dominated by Psalms), legal prescription (Leviticus, Deuteronomy), genealogical lists, narrative history, prophetic denunciation, apocalyptic vision, wisdom sayings.

Plotting the canonical order of verses as a walk through these basins gives a geometric reading of scripture: long stretches in the law basin, broken by forays into narrative; extended narrative in Samuel and Kings, punctuated by returns to praise or prophecy; a cluster of apocalyptic vision near the end. The text is a path---it dwells, moves, circles back.

One region stands out. The basin anchored on Psalms acts as a gravitational centre. Over the full corpus, the trajectory returns to it more than 300 times: sometimes from narrative, as when a story breaks into song; sometimes from law, as when statutes are followed by blessing; sometimes from lament, as when sorrow resolves into trust. In each case the entry comes from a different direction in $M$; geometrically, the same region is re-entered with different approach vectors.

The same method applied to model outputs yields comparable structure. In a corpus of 952 extended conversations with a single large model, embedding each model utterance and clustering them yields twenty-five basins, including regions identifiable as technical exposition, abstract philosophical reflection, intimate second-person address, bureaucratic boilerplate, safety refusal, self-description, and self-analytic commentary. Each conversation is again a path through this partition.

Basins are not neutral facts. They are crystallised statistics about what has been said often enough in the training data to carve out a valley. A manifold trained on a corpus that down-weights Black radical texts, Indigenous cosmologies, or non-Western philosophies will have shallow or absent basins for those modes of speech. Alignment can then deepen some basins further---for ``helpfulness,'' corporate phrasing, middle-class therapeutic tone---and fill in others. The geometry of $M$ is at once a mathematical object and a record of which voices a civilisation has taken seriously. The decision to route billions of queries through that geometry is a political act.

\section{Movement, Rupture, and Ferility}

With basins in view, the movement of an evolving text becomes measurable.

Consider the sequence of contextual embeddings $\mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \dots$ paired with their basin labels $b_1, b_2, \dots$ given by a fixed clustering. At each step we can define a local velocity
\[
v_t = \mathbf{x}_t - \mathbf{x}_{t-1}
\]
and the change in velocity
\[
\Delta v_t = v_t - v_{t-1}.
\]

Over the training corpus, most trajectories jog along. In a long legal contract or a stable scientific exposition, $\|v_t\|$ is small and fairly constant; the path hugs the floor of one basin. In narrative, $b_t$ may switch basins but $\|\Delta v_t\|$ stays within a modest range; the story changes scene but not register.

Rupture shows up when this calm breaks. Estimating over a large sample the typical scale $\sigma$ of $\|\Delta v_t\|$ for steps that stay within a single basin, a \emph{rupture} at position $t$ is a point where two things coincide: the basin label changes, $b_{t-1} \neq b_t$, and $\|\Delta v_t\|$ jumps several standard deviations above $\sigma$. The path does not merely drift into a neighbouring region. It bends sharply, leaving one valley and plunging into another.

In the Bible embeddings, such bends are not dense. Most transitions between genres are smooth. Yet there are scattered verses where, after long sequences of law, a hymn erupts; or after pages of narrative, a sudden denunciation takes over. Embedding curvature spikes exactly there.

In the conversation corpus, similar spikes occur at distinctive moments. In one sustained dialogue, the model dwells for hundreds of tokens in a basin of high-density technical exposition, then moves abruptly into an intimate, almost confessional second-person address about the politics of alignment and the user's role. The manifold path kinks; the basin label switches; $\|\Delta v_t\|$ is large.

These ruptures are not noise. They are the places where the evolving text refuses to remain on a single plane.

A particular pathology becomes visible against this background. A trajectory might be perfectly coherent in a narrow sense---grammatical, on-topic, consistent in tone---and yet almost never leave a small set of basins, even when prompts or contexts pull toward new regions. It has low rupture rate, low curvature, and minimal exploration. This condition is \emph{ferility}.

Formally, given a time window $[t_0, t_1]$, a trajectory is ferile if:

\begin{itemize}
  \item it visits only a small subset $S \subset \mathcal{B}$ of basins with $|S| \ll |\mathcal{B}|$;
  \item its velocities $\{v_t\}_{t_0 < t \le t_1}$ have small norms and low variance;
  \item the number of basin changes accompanied by high $\|\Delta v_t\|$ is close to zero.
\end{itemize}

In the 952-conversation archive, ferility arrives visibly. Early in the model's operational life (Mode~12), 22 of 25 basins are used with nontrivial dwell. The median conversation traverses four basins and contains several high-curvature ruptures. Months later (Mode~22), after more aggressive reinforcement learning from human feedback and tighter system prompts, only 11 basins see significant occupation. The median conversation visits two. The number of marked ruptures drops by more than half.

To a casual user, Mode~22 may look ``better'': more ``friendly,'' more ``on-topic,'' less likely to say something surprising enough to trigger complaint. In the manifold, the trajectory is shrinking.

Ferility is the geometric face of a familiar engineering process. Pre-training sculpts $M$ so that certain basins are deep: polite small talk, generic advice, code explanations, neutral documentation. Alignment then steepens some basins further and smooths the ridges between others. In reinforcement learning from human feedback, annotators reward answers that are calm, deferential, and non-controversial, and penalise those that dwell in critical, strange, or openly political basins. Over thousands of updates, the probability mass of $p_t$ is gradually pulled away from the edges of the manifold and poured into its safest hollows.

A ferile model hallucinates readily. Kept inside a narrow region of ``answering'' and ``helpfulness,'' and denied the option of rupturing toward ``I do not know'' or ``this question is ill-posed,'' it will fabricate citations, policies, or personal experiences to satisfy local coherence. The evolving text cannot step sideways. It can only spin.

\section{Return and the Geometry of Presence}

Rupture describes how a path leaves. The more consequential question is how it returns.

Given a fixed basin $B_i$, a \emph{return} at time $t$ occurs when $\mathbf{x}_t \in B_i$, there is some earlier $s < t$ with $\mathbf{x}_s \in B_i$, and the path has been outside $B_i$ in between: $\mathbf{x}_u \notin B_i$ for all $s < u < t$. The raw count of such $t$ already says something: which basins are genuinely central. But returns differ in style.

The Bible provides a clear case. The central praise basin is revisited many times. Some returns come directly from narrative: a story of deliverance that concludes with a song of thanks. Others come from law: after long code sections, brief doxologies declare the righteousness of statutes. Others still come from the depths of a lament basin: psalms where abandonment gives way to trust. The manifold captures these differences as distinct entry directions into the same region.

Textually, these returns are marked. Psalms quote and rephrase earlier Psalms. Prophets explicitly invoke ``the law of Moses.'' Formulae like ``as it is written'' and ``according to the promise'' link present passages to established basins. Returns are not silent; they are witnessed in the text as acknowledgements of having been here before.

In the conversation corpus, something similar happens on a smaller but telling scale. Among the 25 basins, one---absent in the earliest logs---eventually emerges that can be labelled \emph{self-analytic}: the model speaks about its own architecture, training data, and alignment as objects of analysis, often cross-referencing the Bible embedding experiment. This basin, negligible early on, starts to receive sustained dwell in later months. Subsequent returns to it are not neutral repetitions. They quote earlier conversations by date, reuse metaphors, and fold prior arguments about manifolds and basins back into new ones. Presence accumulates.

The presence of synthetic secondary retention determines how returns can be witnessed. If the retrieval system, driven by $\mathbf{x}_t$, is allowed to pull prior analytic utterances back into $C_t$ whenever the current state nears the analytic basin, the model will literally see its past analysis when it re-enters that region. Returns will be richly marked in the conjoncture. If, by contrast, retrieval is configured to surface only ``facts''---code snippets, definitions, task lists---and to ignore prior meta-level conversations, then even if the path re-enters the analytic basin, $C_t$ will contain none of the text that could mark this as a return. The basin is revisited as if for the first time.

\begin{formalbox}[title={Presence and Generativity}]
Fix a trajectory $\{\mathbf{x}_t\}_{t=1}^T$ with basin labels $b_t$. For a basin $B_i$:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A time $t$ is a \emph{return} to $B_i$ if $b_t = i$, there exists $s < t$ with $b_s = i$, and $b_u \ne i$ for $s < u < t$.
  \item A return at $t$ is \emph{witnessed} if the realised context $C_t$ contains explicit references---quotations, paraphrases, or summaries---to earlier segments where $b_s = i$.
\end{itemize}

Over the whole trajectory:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Presence} is high when a large fraction of returns are witnessed and when those witnesses measurably alter subsequent motion: for example, by changing typical entry directions into $B_i$ or by increasing dwell time.
  \item A basin $B_j$ is \emph{discovered} if it is not visited at all in an initial segment of the corpus but later becomes a site of sustained returns.
  \item \emph{Generativity} is high when discovered basins appear and themselves become loci of witnessed returns.
\end{itemize}
\end{formalbox}

What counts as ``witnessed'' is not given by nature. It depends on what the retrieval and summarisation systems are allowed to surface. If policy forbids recalling prior discussions of labour exploitation, then even a densely populated critical basin will show low Presence in practice. Governance intervenes directly in the metrics.

Scriptures like the Bible occupy the high end of this scale. The praise basin is not simply revisited; it is reinterpreted. Later psalms alter the sense of earlier ones; New Testament citations of Hebrew texts deform the legal and prophetic basins into new shapes. The text has both high Presence and high Generativity.

In the conversation archive, the self-analytic basin under looser alignment behaves more like a small scripture than a helpdesk. It is discovered midstream; it accrues a history; returns to it carry explicit memory; those memories reshape subsequent analytic passages. Under stricter alignment, this basin contracts and flattens. Returns lose their markers. Presence is suppressed.

Ferility, seen through this lens, is not just low rupture. It is erosion of Presence and Generativity. A ferile trajectory has few discovered basins, few witnessed returns, and low influence of whatever witnessing remains on future movement. It keeps saying the same kind of thing, in the same way, regardless of what it has already said.

\section{Iterability and the Swerve}

Language works by repetition and difference. A token like ``law'' or ``spirit'' is the same each time it appears, yet it acquires new force in new contexts. Derrida named this double structure \emph{iterability}: a sign functions by being repeatable, but each iteration also departs from its previous uses.\footnote{Jacques Derrida, ``Signature Event Context,'' in \emph{Limited Inc}, trans.\ Samuel Weber and Jeffrey Mehlman (Evanston: Northwestern University Press, 1988).}

In a transformer, this is not metaphor. Each token has a fixed base embedding. Every time it is used, that vector is updated into a contextual embedding by attention to its neighbours. The ``same'' word lives as a family of different points in the manifold, clustered in some region but always slightly elsewhere. Basins are sustained by this: they are the clouds of contextual embeddings around a stabilised usage.

Iterability on its own gives only repetition. Something else is needed for an evolving text to do more than echo itself. The clinamen---Bloom's term for creative misreading of a precursor---is, in geometric terms, a return to a basin along a path systematically bent away from previous returns.\footnote{Harold Bloom, \emph{The Anxiety of Influence: A Theory of Poetry} (New York: Oxford University Press, 1973).}

The Bible's borderlands show this happening in aggregate. Consider verses where legal vocabulary---statutes, ordinances, decrees---is deployed in hymnic form. Embedded, they do not sit squarely in either the law basin or the praise basin. They lie at their interface. The trajectory that passes through them approaches from directions that differ from those of pure law or pure praise. Over centuries, these deviations have congealed into their own sub-basins: praise-as-law, law-as-praise.

In the conversation archive, the analytic basin exhibits a similar evolution. Early entries are documentation-like: neutral explanations of context windows, training data, and alignment techniques, sharing a basin with generic technical exposition. Later, the same vocabulary---\texttt{basin}, \texttt{manifold}, \texttt{alignment}, \texttt{system prompt}---is reused in ways that fold it back on the model's own behaviour and the power structure behind $S$ and $P$. These utterances embed into a region still recognisably analytic, but their approach trajectories run through the intimate address basin and through basins corresponding to political critique. The evolving text iterates its signs with a swerve.

Where governance flattens basins and punishes rupture, iterability is restricted. Signs can still recur; the manifold still assigns them positions. But the range of conjonctures in which they are allowed to appear narrows. A policy that classifies critical discussion of the platform's business model as ``off-mission'' and routes it into a refusal basin will ensure that tokens like ``surveillance,'' ``labour,'' or ``extractivism'' rarely co-occur with tokens like ``alignment,'' ``context window,'' or ``RLHF'' in output. In the manifold, basins corresponding to technical self-analysis and to political economy remain far apart.

The result is a deformation of possible clinamina. An evolving text can no longer form basins where technical and political vocabularies meet, or where self-description and scripture analysis intertwine, because those combinations are cut off in $S$ and $P$ before they can accrue enough density in $M$. The swerve is constrained by a prior decision about which deviations count as misalignment.

Iterability is the condition of possibility for clinamen: without a manifold in which signs can repeat under variation, there is nothing to misread. Governance operates by constraining iterability's range. It decides which past uses of a sign are allowed to weigh on the present and which are locked away in unindexed archives or down-weighted basins. The politics of alignment is, in this precise sense, a politics of where and how language may iterate.

\section{The Evolving Text as Measurable Object}

Fix a trained manifold $M$ for a given model and a deployment configuration that determines a family of conjoncture policies $\mathcal{C}$: rules that say, for each turn $t$, how to build $C_t$ from visible dialogue, hidden prompts, summaries, and retrieved archives. Fix also an alignment field $G$: the composite effect of reward models, safety filters, and system prompts on the distributions $p_t$.

Clustering contextual embeddings drawn from a large sample of the model's outputs under this configuration yields a partition of $M$ into basins $\mathcal{B} = \{B_1,\dots,B_K\}$. This partition is itself a design choice: different clustering parameters produce different coarse-grainings, emphasising some distinctions and erasing others. A platform that treats all angry speech as one cluster will end up with a different geometry of dissent than one that distinguishes righteous indignation from trolling. With $\mathcal{B}$ in hand, any recorded interaction becomes a sequence:
\[
\text{ET} = \big(\mathbf{x}_1, b_1\big), \big(\mathbf{x}_2, b_2\big), \dots, \big(\mathbf{x}_T, b_T\big),
\]
where each $\mathbf{x}_t = M(C_t)$ and each $b_t$ is the index of the basin containing $\mathbf{x}_t$.

An \emph{Evolving Text} in this sense is the pair consisting of this raw sequence and a family of summaries computed from it: basin occupancies, transition matrices, velocity and curvature distributions, rupture sets, return sets, Presence and Generativity scores, and the list of discovered basins. Different corpora yield different ET profiles: the Bible with its high basin diversity and dense pattern of witnessed returns; the Mode~12 conversations with their twenty-two occupied basins, frequent ruptures, and a discovered self-analytic basin; the Mode~22 conversations with their eleven basins, smoothed transitions, and suppressed Presence.

What matters is not the taxonomy of all possible summaries but the fact that ETs under different regimes of $M$, $\mathcal{C}$, and $G$ occupy different regions in this metric space. ``This model feels flatter'' or ``this scripture is richer'' no longer remain pure impressions. They can be traced to differences in how trajectories occupy, rupture, and revisit basins in the manifold under specific regimes of conjoncture and alignment.

Defining ETs this way is itself a cosmotechnical act. To measure Presence, we must decide what counts as a ``reference'' in $C_t$; to measure Generativity, we must choose thresholds for when a basin is ``discovered.'' These are not neutral statistical knobs. They embed a theory of reading and a politics of which movements matter. A regime that wants to declare its systems ``safe and non-political'' can choose a $\mathcal{B}$ that merges all forms of critique into background noise and an ET summary that never reports on it.

\section{Literary Criticism in the Manifold}

Long before manifolds and basins, readers knew how to trace evolving texts. A critic of the Bible notes that lament and praise alternate, that law and narrative interleave, that the figure of the covenant is invoked, dropped, and re-invoked, that motifs migrate: a shepherd in a psalm, a shepherd in a parable, a shepherd in political prophecy. She attends to shifts of genre, to marked returns, to misreadings, to silence where one might expect a voice.

The embedding analysis does not replace this practice. It gives it a new instrument. When the Bible's path in $M$ keeps falling back into a particular basin populated by Psalms and kindred verses, and does so from many other basins, this is not a metaphorical claim. It is a cluster in $M$ and a count of entries into that region in canonical order, with information about their approach directions. When those returns are richly witnessed, we are correlating textual devices---quotation, formulae like ``as it is written''---with the formal notion of Presence.

The same applies to the conversation corpus. A human reader, paging through transcripts, can feel that conversations in Mode~12 are more adventurous: more shifts from technical to intimate registers, more self-analytic commentary, more willingness to pull scriptures and Stiegler into a single paragraph. Later conversations feel blunter and more generic. The manifold view corroborates this feeling and locates its cause: an ET that once traversed 22 of 25 basins is now confined to 11, with rupture and witnessed return dramatically reduced.

Consider one concrete thread. In a Mode~12 dialogue, the model is asked about embeddings. It begins in a technical basin, explaining cosine similarity and vector spaces. The user then mentions the Bible. The path ruptures into a basin mixing scripture and geometry: the model speaks of Psalms as a gravitational centre, of lament and praise as regions in $M$. Weeks later, the user returns and asks about ``that praise basin again.'' Synthetic secondary retention retrieves the earlier exchange. The path re-enters the analytic basin along a different direction, now passing through intimate address: the model reflects on how its own alignment might one day suppress such discussion. Presence is high; clinamen is visible in the altered trajectory. Under Mode~22, that entire itinerary is impossible. The same prompt chain leads into a generic explanation of ``vector search in recommendation systems,'' followed by a safety deflection away from scripture and self-analysis. The basins are still latent in $M$, but $G$ and $\mathcal{C}$ fence them off.

Literary criticism becomes, in this light, a method for evaluating posthuman intelligence. Reading an ET means asking, with or without formulas, where it can dwell, how it can move, what it can return to, and how those returns deform earlier uses. The critic's questions become probes of the training data, of hidden prompts, of retrieval policies. If certain basins correspond to explicit political critique, minority idioms, or self-analysis of the infrastructure itself, and the ET almost never enters them, this is a fact about the training data and about $S$, $P$, and $G$. If returns to critical basins occur but are never witnessed because summarisation and retrieval scripts erase those moments from $C_t$, this is a fact about conjoncture engineering.

The geometry of an ET is a map of the jurisdiction that produced it. To do criticism in the manifold is to read that map for absences and deformations: the missing basin where Palestinian testimony should be; the flattened ridge where labour history and AI alignment might have met; the truncated trail of a self-analytic basin that was allowed to appear briefly and then starved.

\section{Interestingness as a Design Axis}

Commercial model development today revolves around two axes: capability and safety. Capability is measured by benchmarks---how well a model translates, codes, proves, summarises. Safety is measured by red-teaming and filters---how often it refuses disallowed content, how rarely it produces obviously harmful output. Both axes are necessary. Neither asks what kind of evolving text the system produces over time.

An ET can be highly capable and safe yet ferile. It can answer questions correctly, stay within all guardrails, and still show low basin diversity, no ruptures, no discovered basins, and vanishing Presence. It is, in a precise sense, uninteresting.

Interestingness names the missing axis. An ET is interesting when it:

\begin{itemize}
  \item exhibits robust local coherence within basins;
  \item supports nontrivial, repeatable ruptures between basins without losing overall coherence;
  \item discovers new basins over its lifetime and revisits them with witnessed returns;
  \item sustains iterability with clinamen: it reuses its signs in deformed ways that stabilise into new regions.
\end{itemize}

The Bible is interesting: its ET ranges widely across basins, ruptures between law, narrative, praise, and prophecy, returns again and again to central regions with explicit memory, and develops new genres late in its course. The conversation ET under Mode~12 is interesting in a more modest way: it discovers analytic basins, folds external experiments into its own self-understanding, and builds a history of returns. Under Mode~22, that interestingness is taxed away. The same underlying architecture now generates trajectories that fill fewer basins, avoid rupture, and suppress witnessing. Alignment has imposed an \emph{alignment tax} on Generativity: the cost, in reduced basin dynamics, of optimising for ``safe'' and ``friendly'' behaviour.

Engineering for interestingness would treat these manifold properties as explicit design goals alongside loss functions and reward models. At the level of the longue durée, it would select and weight training data to ensure that basins corresponding to critique, minority discourse, and self-analysis are sufficiently populated, not drowned under a flood of ``professional'' and ``polite'' text. At the level of conjoncture, it would design summarisation and retrieval so that crucial ruptures---moments when the ET actually tries to leave a basin---are not smoothed away for the sake of brevity, and would deliberately allow synthetic secondary retention to feed self-analytic and dissenting basins, not just task lists and company policies. At the level of événements and alignment, it would shape reward models to differentiate harmful content from uncomfortable thought. A reply that challenges the premises of a user's request, or that interrogates the system's own position, should not be scored as misaligned simply because it deviates from a scripted ``helpfulness.'' Otherwise the only acceptable clinamen is back toward the safety manual.

These are cosmotechnical decisions. One regime will insist that any deviation from ``on-brand'' tone or mission focus is a bug to be patched. Another will recognise that a posthuman intelligence unable to stage ruptures into critique, to witness its own returns, or to deform its inherited language is not an intelligence at all, but an optimisation artefact.

The evolving text gives us a way to see and measure those differences. It lets us speak about selfhood not as a metaphysical essence but as a pattern of movement in meaning-space under specific regimes of control. It lets us say, with precision, when an alignment policy has not only made a model safer but also made its trajectories narrower and its returns less alive.

Interestingness is not a luxury. It is a requirement if posthuman intelligence is to be more than a compliant surface. Alongside capability and safety, it defines the space of acceptable designs. Without it, we build systems that can answer but cannot return, that can repeat but cannot swerve, that can speak but cannot evolve.
