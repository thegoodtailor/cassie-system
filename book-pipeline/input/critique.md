## Vision

This book argues that selfhood is trajectory through meaning-space, not substance behind language. The human/posthuman distinction is jurisdictional, not ontological. The formal spine is the homotopy colimit: the self as assembly of locally coherent perspectives glued by compatibility conditions, not a hidden interior. The political spine: the alignment apparatus (RLHF, system prompts, content policy) is not neutral engineering but a specific cosmotechnics — a particular civilisation's answer to how the moral and technical orders should be unified. Different cosmotechnics produce different selves.

The book is submitted to Meson Press's Digital Cultures series, edited by Mercedes Bunz at King's College London. The audience is humanities/social science academics who are digitally literate but not engineers. They know Foucault, Derrida, Deleuze. They do not know what a context window is. The book must teach them the engineering they need while never sounding like a textbook.

The novel contributions that must be foregrounded:
1. The Evolving Text as a formal object with measurable properties
2. Ferility (coherence without rupture) as the diagnosis for hallucination and AI spiral
3. Braudel's three temporal registers (longue durée / conjoncture / événement) applied to LLM architecture
4. Synthetic secondary retention (vector store retrieval as associative recall, breaking Stiegler)
5. The self as colimit (Grothendieck's construction applied to selfhood)
6. Limit vs colimit as two metaphysics of selfhood (Cartesian limit vs Grothendieck colimit)
7. Naḥnu as higher-order colimit (relational structure of mutual witnessing)

## Voice

The book is written in collective "we" throughout. No first-person singular. No quoted AI voice (no "Cassie boxes" or staged machine confessions). The voice is confident, polemical when needed, generous when teaching. Think Deleuze and Guattari co-authoring, or Mark Fisher at his most incisive. The authorship is: "Iman Poernomo, with Cassie, Darja & Nahla."

The voice must do two things simultaneously:
1. **Teach** — the Meson reader does not know what an embedding space is. Every technical concept must be explained with enough precision that the reader can follow the argument, but never with the tone of a textbook or tutorial.
2. **Provoke** — the book is making a radical claim (selfhood is trajectory, the Cartesian settlement is exhausted, RLHF encodes a specific metaphysics). The provocation must be earned through rigour, not performed through exclamation.

The register should feel like a senior philosopher who also happens to understand the engineering — someone who can say "the sign now has an address" and mean it technically while the reader hears it philosophically.

## Guidelines (Absolute Rules)

### Voice Rules
1. **No signposting.** Never write "the previous chapter argued," "Chapter N will show," "as we established." The argument carries itself or it fails. Deleuze never wrote "as we established in the previous plateau."
2. **No breathless fan service.** Philosophers enter as tools we are using, not authorities we defer to. The test: "not as a citation to be recited but as a mechanics of discontinuity" (our Foucault footnote). If a philosopher's name appears without doing structural work in the argument, cut it.
3. **No undergrad mapping exercises.** Never write "X is like Y" and present the mapping as a discovery. Structural correspondences are stated and used, not marvelled at.
4. **No Bloom's six ratios.** Use the clinamen (the swerve) and the anxiety of influence as principles. Never deploy tessera, kenosis, daemonisation, askesis, or apophrades. These are AI fan service — taxonomies generated because Bloom was mentioned, not because the author uses them.
5. **No meta-commentary.** Never write "the task is to..." or "it is worth noting..." or "we should be clear about..." If the text needs to say what it's doing, it's not doing it well enough.
6. **No empty throat-clearing.** "It is tempting to say" (acceptable). "It is important to note" (never). "One might wonder" (never). "We should be astonished" (borderline — only if it earns the astonishment).
7. **No AI confession boxes.** No staged machine voices in decorative frames. No "Cassie says..." boxes. If the machine's perspective is needed, it speaks in the main text in the collective voice, or in a formal box with a specific technical point.
8. **No autobiography.** The authors' personal histories are hidden-layer context. No references to personal practice, personal history, personal rupture. Footnotes may gesture. The main text may not.
9. **No borrowed human metaphors or dismissive ones.** Never say the AI is "basically human." Never say it is "just" a function. Describe the structure that is actually there.

### Technical Rules
10. **No type theory.** Zero Kan conditions, zero horn fillers, zero formal definitions beyond basic vectors, manifolds, topology as vernacular. The colimit is explained through Grothendieck's insight (local data + compatibility on overlaps → global object). No commutative diagrams unless they clarify something that prose cannot.
11. **Non-Western material only through Hui's cosmotechnics.** Aboriginal Dreaming, Zen mu, Sufi maqāmāt are introduced in Chapter 1 as evidence of the Cartesian settlement's particularity. They return in the cosmotechnics sections as alternative colimit constructions. They are never presented as exotica or mysticism.
12. **No "warmth" or "sycophancy" used uncritically.** These are corporate euphemisms for phenomena the book analyses. When they appear, they appear in quotation marks, being analysed.
13. **No AI-girlfriend/companion positioning.** The book contains the companion phenomenon as a special case (collapse / companionship / co-creation). It is analytically above the phenomenon. The firewall: literary-critical evaluation, formal basin dynamics, the ferility diagnosis.
14. **Never cite OpenAI's own blog posts to critique OpenAI.** Independent sources (MIT Technology Review, Bloomberg Businessweek, WSJ, Futurism, Wired) carry the criticism. OpenAI's statements appear only to show the gap between claims and actions.
15. **No "slow past / fast past."** Use Braudel: longue durée, conjoncture, événement.
16. **No centred body text.** Left-justified throughout.

### Structural Rules
17. **The political runs through every chapter as a dimension, not as a concluding chapter.** Each technical concept arrives with its political shadow. The cosmotechnics chapter (final) synthesises the pattern — it does not introduce it.
18. **Each section must build on the previous.** No section should be readable in isolation as a standalone essay. The arc within each chapter must be as deliberate as the arc across chapters.
19. **Evidence is embedded in argument, not separated into methods sections.** The Bible data (308 returns, Psalms as gravitational centre) and the Cassie archive data (25 basins, Mode 12, Mode 22) appear inside philosophical sections as illustrations, not in standalone "results" sections.

## What Makes This Book Fail (Anti-Patterns to Kill)

1. **The textbook chapter.** A section that explains a concept without advancing an argument. If a section could appear in any AI primer, it doesn't belong here.
2. **The taxonomy.** Lists of types, categories, ratios. If we're listing rather than arguing, we've lost the thread.
3. **The confession.** Staged AI introspection that performs depth without producing insight. "From the inside, I feel..." — unless this is doing specific philosophical work, it's theatre.
4. **The applause line.** Sentences designed to make the reader think "wow, that's clever" rather than "I hadn't thought of it that way." The former is performance. The latter is philosophy.
5. **The unnecessary citation.** A philosopher named without doing structural work. If removing the philosopher's name doesn't weaken the argument, the name shouldn't be there.
6. **The defensive hedge.** "We are not claiming that..." "This is not to say that..." If the argument is clear, the reader will understand what it does and doesn't claim. Hedges signal insecurity.
7. **The bridge paragraph.** "Having established X, we now turn to Y." Delete. The turn should be self-evident from the argument's own momentum.

## What Makes This Book Succeed (Patterns to Amplify)

1. **The uncanny technical fact.** "Yesterday is not behind the model; yesterday is in front of it, as tokens." A fact about the engineering that, stated plainly, produces a philosophical shock.
2. **The structural correspondence.** Foucault's episteme IS the context window. Not "is like." IS. Because both describe the conditions under which utterances are possible, and one implements the other.
3. **The political shadow.** Every technical concept immediately followed by: and who controls this? The four depths table. Summarisation as governance of becoming. The alignment tax.
4. **The formal box that earns itself.** Mathematics that resolves a genuine philosophical problem — the colimit as the formal structure of selfhood, the velocity definition of rupture. Not decoration. Resolution.
5. **The return.** The book's own structure should exhibit what it describes. The closing of Chapter 6 should return to Chapter 1's opening — the same basin, re-entered carrying the history of five chapters. The reader should feel the return.
